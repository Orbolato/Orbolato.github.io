Planning vertiport infrastructure from existing helipad sites: A multi-scale case study in São Paulo State

Can an existing helipad inventory support strategic planning of urban air mobility ground infrastructure across different geographic scales?
Rodrigo Mollo Furlan · Lucas Orbolato Carvalho · Marcelo Xavier Guterres · Anderson Ribeiro Correia
Aeronautics Institute of Technology (ITA), São José dos Campos, Brazil
Publisher / DOIGitHub repositoryAuthor homepage

Study at a glance

A capacitated location model evaluates registered helipads as candidate vertiport sites in São Paulo, Campinas, Guarulhos, and across São Paulo State. The screening jointly considers population-weighted planning demand, candidate site capacities, geometric access restrictions, a minimum candidate-reachable area target, and a soft spacing preference. Exact optimization is used at smaller scales; variable neighborhood search (VNS) and an SMA-inspired binary search/VNS relay support statewide screening.

These scenarios use distinct demand intensities, access radii, coverage floors, and geographic supports. Selected-site counts should not be interpreted as a controlled measure of scale effects.

Reachable coverage is not statewide coverage

All five statewide relay runs satisfied the target covering roughly 80% of the area geometrically reachable from the entire candidate inventory. However, the selected sites covered only about 14.34% of the total state grid area within the assumed 10-km territorial radius.

The distinction is central to the policy interpretation: satisfying a candidate-relative coverage target does not imply that most of the state can be reached. Additional sites or different assumptions would be required for broader geographic coverage.

Sensitivity and practical implications

Demand and spatial representation

The study varies demand intensity across five seeds and tests concentrated versus dispersed population-weighted demand. Grid subdivision (1 km, 500 m, and 250 m) and boundary-distance diagnostics show that configurations near coverage thresholds can change feasibility under different spatial representations.

Optimization and implementation

Exact optimization was practical for the municipal and pooled regional cases. Statewide stochastic search enables computational screening, but a five-seed comparison does not establish uniform superiority of the relay approach over VNS. Helipad registration alone does not establish ownership, site suitability, charging capacity, airspace feasibility, or service readiness.

Code, data, and reproducibility

The author-provided computational package contains the location model, search algorithms, saved site selections and allocations, derived ANAC/IBGE inputs, spatial sensitivity experiments, and generated tables and figures.
