Data-driven framework for urban air mobility planning: Integrating socioeconomic and operational factors for optimal network design in São Paulo, Brazil

Lucas Orbolato Carvalho · Igor Galhano Gomes · Jaqueline Fonseca Rodrigues · Mayara Condé Rocha Murça · Marcelo Xavier Guterres · Daniel Alberto Pamplona

Publication

Case Studies on Transport Policy, Volume 25, Article 101848 (2026). DOI: 10.1016/j.cstp.2026.101848.

Publisher / DOIResearchGateAuthor homepage

Study summary

This case study develops a data-driven framework for initial Urban Air Mobility network planning in the São Paulo Metropolitan Region. It integrates socioeconomic demand information, existing helipad infrastructure, vertiport siting, origin-destination demand estimation, route design and operational constraints. Partitioning Around Medoids clustering is used to identify candidate vertiport locations, while the existing helicopter route structure is reused to construct conflict- and distance-minimized connections compatible with an incremental deployment strategy. The resulting network supports an illustrative daily demand of 186 UAM operations and emphasizes infrastructure reuse as a way to reduce initial implementation barriers. The framework is intended as a transferable planning methodology rather than a forecast of realized UAM demand, and it highlights the interaction between market viability, aeronautical infrastructure and airspace constraints in early network design.

Keywords: urban air mobility; vertiport location; route optimization; demand estimation; airspace design; clustering.
